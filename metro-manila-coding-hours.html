Quick Answer: Manila Coding Hours Today

The standard MMDA schedule is 7:00 AM to 10:00 AM in the morning and 5:00 PM to 8:00 PM in the evening, Monday to Friday. The window hours are 10:01 AM to 4:59 PM, when a coded car can travel on covered roads. There is no coding from 8:01 PM to 6:59 AM overnight, and there is no regular coding on Saturday, Sunday or a public holiday.

What Is Number Coding in Manila?

Number coding, also called color coding, is the MMDA traffic scheme that keeps about one in five private vehicles off major roads during weekday rush hours. It started in 1995 under MMDA Regulation No. 96-005, and it took its current two block form in August 2022 when the expanded UVVRP was reimposed. The aim is to cut traffic volume on roads such as EDSA, C-5, Commonwealth Avenue, Roxas Boulevard, Quezon Avenue, Ortigas Avenue and Marcos Highway.

The scheme applies to private cars on MMDA covered national roads and major thoroughfares. Each city then applies it on its own streets by local ordinance, which is why most cities copy the MMDA hours while Makati runs its own longer hours. The rule follows the road you are on, not the city where you live or where your car is registered. That point matters on EDSA, because the same road passes through several cities with different local rules.

What Are Coding Window Hours?

Coding window hours are the free period between the morning and evening bans. In Metro Manila the window is 10:01 AM to 4:59 PM, Monday to Friday, on roads that follow the MMDA scheme. A coded car may travel freely during this window, even on its coding day. The window is not a grace period at the edge of rush hour, and it is not a suspension of coding for the whole day.

The window makes coding workable for most drivers. You can do a midday errand, a school run after 10:00 AM, or a trip that starts after the morning rush and ends before 5:00 PM. The risk is the exact start and end time, because the clock is strict. A coded car on EDSA at 10:00 AM is still inside the morning block, while the same car at 10:01 AM is inside the window. The same strict line applies at 5:00 PM, when the evening block starts.

Remember: Window hours apply where the city follows the MMDA scheme as it stands. Makati city roads do not have window hours, and some local roads may have their own rules, so check the road you plan to use.

Does EDSA Have Window Hours?

Yes, window hours apply on EDSA. EDSA is an MMDA covered national road, so the MMDA scheme applies there, including the 10:01 AM to 4:59 PM window from Monday to Friday. A coded car can use EDSA during the window, and it cannot use EDSA during the 7:00 to 10:00 AM and 5:00 to 8:00 PM blocks on its coding day.

This point causes confusion because Makati has different hours, and EDSA passes through Makati. The MMDA has stated that the EDSA stretch follows the expanded MMDA scheme with window hours, while Makati city streets follow the city ordinance. To avoid a ticket, treat EDSA as an MMDA road and treat the streets inside Makati as Makati roads. For a focused check, see this guide to EDSA coding hours before you enter EDSA on your coding day.

Makati Coding Hours: The Big Exception

Makati: 7:00 AM to 7:00 PM, no window hours. Makati City enforces number coding on its local roads from 7:00 AM to 7:00 PM, Monday to Friday, with no window hours. That is a continuous 12 hour ban for a coded car on Makati city roads. A plate ending in 1 or 2 is coded all day Monday in Makati, and a midday trip that is legal in Quezon City can be a ticket in Makati.

Makati runs its own ordinance, so its hours do not follow the MMDA window. The main roads inside Makati that drivers name most often are Ayala Avenue, Gil Puyat Avenue, and Chino Roces Avenue. Toll roads above the city, such as the Skyway, are outside the city ordinance. The MMDA scheme was suspended on some dates in 2026 for holidays and special events, and Makati follows the same holiday suspension, so always confirm the latest MMDA advisory before a holiday trip.

Coding Hours by City in Metro Manila

Most cities in Metro Manila follow the standard MMDA schedule on covered roads. That includes Manila, Quezon City, Caloocan, Malabon, Valenzuela, Mandaluyong, Pasig, San Juan, Pasay, Paranaque, Las Pinas, Muntinlupa, Taguig, Marikina and Navotas. In each of these cities, the coding hours are 7:00 to 10:00 AM and 5:00 to 8:00 PM, with window hours from 10:01 AM to 4:59 PM.

The difference is not the city name alone, but the road type inside the city. A national road such as EDSA, C-5 or Commonwealth Avenue follows the MMDA hours in every city it passes through. A small local street may follow a city ordinance instead, and a few cities apply coding only on certain major roads. If your route mixes a national road and a Makati street, plan for the stricter Makati rule for the Makati part of the trip.

Which Vehicles Are Exempt From Number Coding?

Not every vehicle follows the UVVRP. The MMDA exemption list covers public utility vehicles, transport network vehicle services such as Grab, motorcycles, garbage trucks, marked government vehicles, fuel trucks, marked media vehicles, fire trucks, ambulances, and vehicles carrying perishable or essential goods. These vehicles can use covered roads during coding hours when they meet the stated conditions for their category.

Electric vehicles have a separate exemption under the Electric Vehicle Industry Development Act, or RA 11697. Registered battery electric vehicles are exempt from number coding until April 15, 2030, subject to the applicable requirements. Hybrid and plug in hybrid vehicles may also be exempt when they have the required classification and MMDA exemption sticker. A mild hybrid should not be assumed to be exempt, because its classification is different, so check the official classification before you rely on it.

How Much Is the Fine for Number Coding in 2026?

Standard fine: ₱500

The standard fine under the Metro Manila Traffic Code of 2023 and the Single Ticketing System is 500 pesos per number coding violation. That is the harmonized amount that applies across Metro Manila under the current code. An unpaid ticket can lead to a surcharge and an LTO alarm, which can block vehicle registration renewal until the fine is settled.

Older guides still quote 300 pesos, which was the MMDA fine under the original 1996 regulation before the 2023 code. That is why you will see both amounts online, and the 500 peso figure is the current one to use for 2026. You normally have 10 business days from the date of apprehension to settle a Single Ticketing System citation, and payment channels include GCash and Maya for government bills. Always keep the citation number, because you need it to pay or to contest the ticket.

How to Check If You Are Coded Today

You can check your status in less than a minute if you follow the same steps each time. Do not rely on memory alone, because the plate day and the time both matter.

  • Look at the last digit of your plate number and find your coding day in the table above.
  • Check the day of the week and the current time against the 7:00 to 10:00 AM and 5:00 to 8:00 PM blocks.
  • Check if today is a weekend, a public holiday, or a day the MMDA has suspended coding.
  • Check your route, because Makati city roads have no window hours even when other cities do.
  • If you are inside a coding block on your coding day, wait for the 10:01 AM to 4:59 PM window or travel after 8:00 PM.

Can I Drive on My Coding Day?

Yes, you can still drive on your coding day, but you must avoid covered roads during the two banned periods. Most drivers use one of three safe periods on their coding day, and they plan the return trip as carefully as the outward trip.

  • Travel before 7:00 AM, and finish the trip before the morning block starts.
  • Travel during the window hours, from 10:01 AM to 4:59 PM, and be off the covered road before 5:00 PM.
  • Travel after 8:00 PM, when the evening block has ended.
  • Use a toll expressway where coding does not apply, and remember that coding resumes when you exit onto a covered road.
  • Use public transport, a TNVS ride, or another car that is not coded that day if your trip must fall inside a banned period.

Is There Number Coding on Weekends and Holidays?

No, there is no regular number coding on Saturday, Sunday and Philippine public holidays. The MMDA also suspends coding for special events and severe weather, and it announces those suspensions in advance. In 2026, coding was lifted for dates such as Eid al Fitr in March, Labour Day in May, Eid al Adha in May and Independence Day in June, and for November 16 to 18, 2026 during the ASEAN Summit special non working holidays.

A suspension is different from window hours. A suspension cancels coding for the whole day, while window hours are part of the normal weekday schedule. If you are unsure about a specific date, check the latest MMDA announcement before you drive, because a temporary suspension or a local traffic rule can change the normal schedule for a specific road.
